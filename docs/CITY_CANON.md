# City Canon and Map Foundation

This document is the authoritative reference for the fictional city in which Outbreak takes place. Use it when creating world maps, neighborhoods, mission locations, landmarks, infrastructure, factions, quests, environmental storytelling, and outbreak history.

`CURRENT_BUILD.md` remains authoritative for what the playable prototype currently implements. This document defines world canon and map requirements; it does not imply that every described location or system already exists in the game.

## Canon Status Labels

- **Confirmed:** Established world canon. Do not contradict it without an explicit revision.
- **Map requirement:** A constraint that future city and mission maps must satisfy.
- **Planned:** Approved direction that is not yet implemented or fully designed.
- **Pending:** Intentionally unresolved. Do not invent a permanent answer without a dedicated decision.
- **Superseded:** Rejected former canon that must not return as current lore.

## Core Identity

### Confirmed

- Outbreak takes place in a fictional city in the Midwestern United States.
- The city has a pre-outbreak municipal population of approximately **411,000 residents**.
- It is a fast-growing regional city rather than a major metropolis.
- Despite its population, the city retains a strong small-city identity: residents commonly think in terms of neighborhoods, local institutions, and familiar commercial areas rather than an anonymous urban mass.
- The setting is intended to provide the isolated regional-city tension associated with classic survival horror while remaining an original city with its own geography, institutions, and history.
- The city name, state, county, demonym, founding date, and exact outbreak year are pending.

### Superseded

- Brasília is no longer the setting.
- Distrito Federal population data and administrative regions are not part of the city canon.
- SIA, Guará, Sudoeste/Octogonal, Cruzeiro, Asa Sul, Asa Norte, Metrô-DF, and Brasília-specific landmarks must not appear as in-world locations.
- Brasília may remain an out-of-universe visual reference for placing a major body of water between contrasting urban districts, but its street plan and proper nouns are not canon.

## Regional Geography

### Confirmed

- The city occupies a broad basin enclosed on most sides by a mountain range.
- The mountains are a natural regional barrier, not a continuous impassable wall.
- A large artificial reservoir occupies much of the northern portion of the municipal area.
- The reservoir was created by a major dam.
- The primary commercial and civic city lies along the reservoir's southern shore.
- The opposite northern shore contains the city's most affluent residential area, including gated communities, large homes, private amenities, and waterfront property.
- The reservoir, mountains, and limited transportation corridors make a rapid quarantine plausible.

### Map requirements

- The reservoir should occupy roughly the northern third of the future city map and remain recognizable at every map scale.
- The dam should sit at an eastern or northeastern constriction where the reservoir drains out of the basin. Its exact orientation remains pending until the macro map is drawn.
- The mountain range must contain a limited number of credible passes, service roads, trails, drainage routes, and utility corridors.
- The city must have multiple escape possibilities before the quarantine, but few enough that military containment remains feasible.
- The reservoir must have defined shorelines, boat access points, service areas, and downstream flow. It cannot function as an undefined decorative lake.
- Bridges and shoreline roads must connect districts logically. Their number and placement must be fixed before neighborhood maps are finalized.
- Any future flood, dam failure, or deliberate demolition is non-canon until separately approved.

## Macro Layout

The future city map should be organized around the following functional zones. These are canonical roles, but their final names and exact boundaries remain pending.

### 1. Southern Commercial Core

- Located on the southern shore of the reservoir.
- Contains the downtown skyline, office buildings, hotels, banks, restaurants, major retail, and dense mixed-use blocks.
- Contains the primary civic district or directly borders it.
- Serves as the central interchange for roads, buses, and the future metro network.
- Should contain valuable supplies and important records but become one of the most congested and dangerous outbreak zones.

### 2. Civic and Emergency District

- Located within or beside the commercial core.
- Contains city hall, courts, emergency-management offices, the major police headquarters, and central public-service buildings.
- Provides a credible location for official outbreak records, failed emergency coordination, police operations, and government cover-up evidence.
- Must have road access appropriate for emergency vehicles and prisoner transport.

### 3. Northern Affluent Shore

- Located across the reservoir from the commercial core.
- Contains luxury neighborhoods, gated communities, waterfront estates, a marina or yacht club, private recreation, and expensive retail or hospitality.
- Uses lower-density development and more controlled access than the southern city.
- Should not be uniformly safe or morally homogeneous. Wealth allowed some residents to prepare or evacuate early, while private security, concealed supplies, abandoned staff housing, and blocked gates create distinct dangers.

### 4. Older Residential Belt

- Contains established working- and middle-class neighborhoods built before the city's recent expansion.
- Includes houses, apartment buildings, schools, churches, local markets, pharmacies, clinics, small police stations, fire stations, garages, and neighborhood businesses.
- Provides the most common environment for civilian stories, survivor shelters, household weapons, and mixed everyday loot.
- Street layouts may be less orderly than newer developments but must remain navigable and internally consistent.

### 5. Western Growth Corridor

- Represents the city's recent expansion.
- Contains newer subdivisions, construction sites, shopping centers, large retailers, unfinished roads, utility work, and commuter development.
- Provides a logical source for building materials, tools, temporary fencing, heavy machinery, and incomplete infrastructure.
- At least one large retailer may include a licensed firearm and sporting-goods section.

### 6. Eastern Industrial and Logistics Corridor

- Located near freight rail, highways, utility infrastructure, and the downstream side of the dam.
- Contains warehouses, workshops, distribution centers, freight yards, fuel storage, vehicle depots, and technical facilities.
- Supports industrial loot, construction resources, machinery, fuel, and hazardous environmental storytelling.
- Must have credible separation from dense housing while still employing a large part of the city.

### 7. Medical and Institutional Zone

- Contains at least one major hospital and may include medical offices, laboratories, education, rehabilitation, or long-term-care facilities.
- Does not replace neighborhood clinics; it serves a larger regional population.
- Must be reachable from major roads and public transportation.
- Major hospitals should become high-threat outbreak locations because of crowding, concealed infections, failed triage, and emergency evacuation attempts.

### 8. Reservoir and Dam District

- Includes the dam, hydroelectric or control infrastructure, spillways, maintenance buildings, service tunnels, shoreline facilities, and water-management systems.
- May include parks, boat ramps, fishing areas, maintenance housing, and restricted utility land.
- The dam is both a landmark and a strategic chokepoint.
- Dam interiors, service tunnels, and control rooms may become bespoke late-game locations, but their explorable extent is planned.

### 9. Mountain and Outer-Ring Communities

- Includes low-density housing, cabins, farms, roadside businesses, forest access, communications infrastructure, and isolated utility sites.
- Contains the principal mountain passes and outer highways.
- Civilian firearm ownership and hunting equipment are more common here than in the dense urban core.
- These areas provide possible escape attempts, hidden survivor communities, perimeter encounters, and covert routes without making the quarantine ineffective.

## Population Rules

### Confirmed

- The municipal population immediately before the outbreak is approximately 411,000.
- This number describes the city proper, not an undefined metropolitan region.
- The surrounding county and outlying settlements may add population later, but their totals are pending and must not silently change the city's 411,000 figure.

### Map requirements

- Every finalized neighborhood must receive a population estimate.
- Final neighborhood totals should sum to approximately 411,000, allowing only a small documented rounding difference.
- Dense southern districts should contain more residents than the northern luxury shore.
- Industrial, civic, reservoir, and mountain districts should have lower residential populations than their physical size might suggest.
- Population density must agree with housing type, road capacity, transit access, hospitals, schools, and commercial services.

## Roads, Crossings, and External Access

### Map requirements

- The city must have a limited number of major highways entering through mountain passes or low gaps in the terrain.
- At least one freight-rail corridor must serve the industrial district and connect the city to the outside region.
- A regional airport or municipal airfield is required because a city of this size needs a credible aviation connection. Its exact size and location are pending.
- The reservoir's two inhabited shores require multiple ordinary connections before the outbreak. The final macro map should include:
  - One primary bridge carrying the heaviest daily traffic.
  - At least one secondary road crossing or longer shoreline connection.
  - A restricted dam service crossing that is not a normal substitute for the public bridges.
- Boat ramps, marinas, maintenance docks, and navigable-water exits must be accounted for during quarantine design.
- No road, tunnel, bridge, rail line, mountain pass, airfield, or water exit may disappear from the story when containment begins. Each requires a documented closure, failure, military position, obstruction, or remaining escape risk.

## Metro and Public Transportation

### Confirmed

- The city has a metropolitan rail system.
- The system includes underground stations and tunnels.
- Some stations, platforms, service corridors, maintenance spaces, and restricted infrastructure will eventually be explorable.
- Metro locations are reserved for one of the final major map-design passes.

### Planned constraints

- The system may combine underground central sections with surface or elevated outer sections.
- Its routes must connect major population and employment centers rather than exist only to provide horror environments.
- The commercial core should contain the principal interchange.
- At least one route should serve or approach the northern shore, medical zone, or western growth corridor.
- Industrial access may use a separate freight corridor rather than passenger metro service.
- The system must have a documented reason it cannot provide an easy escape after quarantine. Possible causes include sealed portals, military occupation, derailment, fire, flooding, power loss, structural damage, and heavy infected concentrations. The final cause is pending.
- Metro route names, station count, rolling stock, operating history, and exact tunnel map remain pending.

## Firearms and Civilian Weapon Availability

### Confirmed

- The surrounding state has permissive civilian firearm laws.
- Lawful firearm ownership is common enough that weapons and ammunition can plausibly be found in some homes.
- The city contains dedicated gun stores and sporting-goods businesses.
- Certain large retailers carry firearms and ammunition in licensed sporting-goods departments.
- Police stations and the major headquarters contain secured armories appropriate to their size and function.

### Consistency rules

- Firearms must not appear in every residence.
- Household weapons should reflect owner background, neighborhood, storage practices, and local culture.
- Civilian locations primarily contain civilian-legal handguns, shotguns, hunting rifles, sporting rifles, and compatible ammunition.
- Automatic weapons, explosives, and specialized military equipment remain rare outside military, tactical-police, criminal, or conspiracy-related locations.
- Seven days of panic, looting, defensive use, police action, and failed evacuations should make ammunition valuable even in a firearm-friendly city.
- Gun stores and police armories should be high-risk, heavily contested, partially depleted, locked, damaged, or controlled rather than effortless weapon caches.

## Police and Emergency Services

### Confirmed

- The city has several small police precincts or substations distributed across its neighborhoods.
- One major police headquarters coordinates citywide operations.
- Police locations vary in size, staffing, armory capacity, holding facilities, vehicle access, and surviving evidence.
- Small stations provide localized equipment and neighborhood incident records.
- The headquarters is a major landmark and a future high-threat mission location.

### Map requirements

- Every major residential district should fall within a defined precinct service area.
- Precinct distribution must follow population and travel time rather than placing one building mechanically in every map zone.
- The industrial corridor, affluent shore, commercial core, and outer communities may use different policing arrangements.
- Fire stations, emergency dispatch, and ambulance staging locations must be added during the civic-services pass.
- The final outbreak timeline must explain why decentralized police facilities could not restore coordination after headquarters or communications failed.

## Hospitals and Clinics

### Confirmed

- Small clinics, urgent-care offices, pharmacies, and medical practices are distributed throughout the city.
- Most populated neighborhoods have at least one local medical facility.
- A smaller number of major hospitals serve multiple neighborhoods and the surrounding region.
- The city should have several major hospitals rather than one implausibly universal facility.

### Map requirements

- Hospital placement must correspond to population, road access, and ambulance coverage.
- Major hospitals require emergency entrances, service areas, parking, utility support, and nearby transit or arterial roads.
- Neighborhood clinics should provide ordinary medical loot and localized stories.
- Major hospitals should provide rare supplies, research clues, difficult rescue objectives, and evidence of the outbreak's early amplification.
- Final hospital count, names, specialties, bed capacity, and neighborhood coverage remain pending.

## Safehouse Placement Rules

### Confirmed constraints

- The permanent safehouse is a large, fenced residential property formerly owned by the lead scientist connected to the outbreak.
- It must be close enough to the city for repeated scavenging missions but outside the densest commercial and hospital collapse zones.
- It requires plausible road access, defensive space, multiple rooms, a garage, and room for later expansion.
- Its location should support the established contrast between security, secrecy, and nearby danger.

### Pending

- Whether the safehouse is located on the affluent northern shore, in a secluded mountain-edge neighborhood, or in another low-density district.
- Its exact relationship to the scientist's worksite and escape route.
- Why the selected tutorial companion knows about the property.

## Quarantine Geometry

### Derived constraints

- Mountain passes allow forces to reduce the number of land checkpoints.
- The reservoir divides movement and makes bridges strategic control points.
- The dam and downstream corridor require military or technical control.
- Highways, freight rail, the airport, metro portals, utility tunnels, drainage systems, mountain trails, and watercraft remain possible escape routes and must be addressed.
- The perimeter should be a layered containment system using terrain, roadblocks, existing fences, vehicles, observation posts, patrols, drones, demolition, and controlled access points rather than an instantly constructed wall.
- Imperfect coverage must leave room for covert movement, deserters, smugglers, government teams, and the possibility that infected people escaped before full containment.

## Map-Design Sequence

Future city work should proceed in this order:

1. Choose the city name, state, county, and broad regional identity.
2. Draw the mountain basin, reservoir, dam, downstream river, and municipal boundary.
3. Fix highways, mountain passes, bridges, rail, airport, and shoreline access.
4. Establish the nine macro zones and their boundaries.
5. Divide the populated zones into named neighborhoods.
6. Assign neighborhood populations totaling approximately 411,000.
7. Place police, fire, medical, utility, education, retail, and transport services.
8. Reserve the safehouses, covert facility, faction sites, and major narrative landmarks.
9. Design the quarantine perimeter against every known exit route.
10. Design metro routes and explorable underground locations after the surface city is stable.

## Consistency Checklist

Before approving a new city location, confirm:

- Its district and neighborhood are identified.
- Its purpose matches the surrounding population and economy.
- Roads, transit, utilities, and emergency coverage can plausibly serve it.
- Its loot follows the location's real function.
- Its police and medical coverage agrees with this document.
- Any firearms match civilian, police, criminal, or military availability rules.
- It does not introduce an unaccounted escape route through the quarantine.
- It does not move the reservoir, dam, mountains, bridges, or established districts.
- It does not reuse superseded Brasília geography or statistics.
- Planned content is labeled planned rather than implemented.
